Access Model

Who can do what, and in which complex. A complex is one condo community with its own public website, properties, content and people. One deployment serves many complexes.

1. The roles at a glance

not signed in

Visitor

Browses a complex's public site only.
  • Sees that complex's active, non-private properties
  • Sees its site content, Things To Do and seasons
  • Cannot book (booking requires an account)
role: Guest

Guest / Renter

Self-signs up (emailed code) on one complex's site.
  • Requests bookings, sees own bookings and documents
  • Also sees private properties of an owner who invited them
  • Can sign in only on their own complex's website
  • Cannot enter the admin panel
role: Owner

Owner

Created by a Site Admin or Super Admin. Staff, but scoped.
  • Admin panel for only properties where ownerId = them
  • Their properties' bookings, seasons, add-ons, payment terms, documents
  • Their own invites, trusted renters and Gmail connection
  • Anything else returns 404 (not 403), so other people's ids are never confirmed
role: Site Admin

Site Admin

Runs one complex. Created by a Super Admin.
  • Edits that complex's Site Content and Things To Do
  • Adds, resets, deactivates and removes its Owners (can only create Owners)
  • Sees every property and booking in the complex, but changes only ones they own; booking decisions stay with the property's Owner
  • No Complexes screen, no domains, cannot create Site Admins
  • Never sees another complex (404)
role: SuperAdmin

Super Admin

The platform operator (you and your team). Not tied to a complex.
  • Everything above, in every complex
  • Creates and renames complexes, assigns custom domains, makes Site Admins
  • Moves a person to another role or complex (Users → the person → role and complex)
  • Top-bar complex switcher: "All complexes" or one
  • Sees unassigned properties (ownerId = null)
Where roles live: Super Admin is a platform-level flag on the account. Guest, Owner and Site Admin are a person's membership in one complex. Today a person belongs to one complex, and every email address is unique across the whole platform.

3. How scope flows: who belongs where

Scope is a chain: Complex → Person (membership) → Property (ownerId) → Booking (via its property).

Super Admin — above every complex; creates complexes and their domains
Complex A  Site with domain(s), site content, Things To Do
Site Admin — runs Complex A: content, Things To Do, Owners; sees all of A's properties and bookings
Owner A1
Property 1  ownerId = A1
Seasons, add-ons, payment terms, rental agreement
Bookings → made by Guests; Owner A1 manages them
Property 2 (private)  isPrivate = true
Visible only to Guests holding an active grant from Owner A1
A1's invites, grants, Gmail connection
Owner A2 — same shape; cannot see or touch Owner A1's properties (404)
Complex B — fully separate: its own Site Admin, Owners, properties, content. Nothing in B is visible from A (404).
Unassigned properties (ownerId = null) — Super Admin only

4. Which website a visitor sees

By domain

Each complex has its own domain(s). The address a visitor types decides which complex they see. A domain belongs to exactly one complex.

Preview address

Before a domain exists, <main address>/c/<short name> shows that complex (the link under "Preview site" in Site Content). It is a cookie-based preview, not the real address.

Sign-in is per complex

On a complex's domain only its own members (and Super Admins) can sign in. Anyone else gets the same message as a wrong password.

Property visibility

Inactive: hidden from everyone but those who manage it. Active + public: everyone. Active + private: its Owner, staff who manage it, and Guests with an active grant. The admin panel's list is the only "everything I manage" view; the role still decides authorization.

5. Working in several complexes (Super Admin)

  1. The top-bar switcher chooses All complexes or one complex. The choice is remembered in your browser.
  2. Lists (properties, bookings, users) show everything on All complexes, or just the chosen complex.
  3. Site Content and Things To Do edit one complex's public site, so on "All complexes" they ask you to pick a complex first and show no fields. This stops edits landing on the wrong site.
  4. "Editing <complex>" at the top of those screens shows where your changes go, with Preview site and Live site links.
  5. Site Admins and Owners never see the switcher; they see their complex's name.

6. Account lifecycle rules

  1. Guests sign up themselves with an emailed 6-digit code, on one complex's website.
  2. Owners are never self-service: a Site Admin (for their complex) or a Super Admin creates them and gets a one-time temporary password plus login URL, built from that complex's own domain.
  3. Site Admins are created only by a Super Admin.
  4. Created/reset accounts have mustChangePassword: the API blocks everything except changing the password until done.
  5. isActive = false blocks sign-in and ends existing sessions on the next request.
  6. Sessions are a JWT in an httpOnly cookie; every request re-reads the user from the database, so role and deactivation changes apply immediately.
  7. You cannot demote, deactivate or delete the last active Super Admin, and cannot change your own role.
  8. A person belongs to one complex; every email address is unique across the platform.
